## Reading the spread, not the mean

A quiet book cannot hold one level of interest, and should not try. A lull is fine when the chapter after it lifts and when something in it pays off later. So the questions are: does a lull set something up, and do two lulls run together?

| Lull | What it sets up | Pays off | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00300 | the weed on the third post | 00700 | sets up a payoff |
| 00400 | "and forty"; the late weed | 00700, the best-scored moment | sets up a payoff; **never trim the clue lines** |

**Finding:** 00300 and 00400 are two lulls in a row. Each one is justified alone; together they are the stretch where reader B would most likely drop off. This is a structural note for the writer, not something trimming fixes.

**Recommendation:** keep both clues; give 00300 an outside pressure instead of more incident: the Board's letter (from 00200) unanswered on the shelf, which Agnes asks Wren to read again. *(Proposal.)*

## The blind guesses

Both readers guessed that the tide table is wrong for the island (A from 00400, sure; B from 00700, guessed). The register wants a reader to be able to solve it from 00400, so A's guess is the hook working.
