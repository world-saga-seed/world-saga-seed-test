# The Oil Cart (the writer's first draft, kept as a style sample)

Dell came at low water with the cart and the two barrels, and the pony stopped where it always stopped, at the foot of the gallery steps, and would not come one yard closer to the sea. Mrs. Rook went down to him with her shawl over her head. I carried the funnel.

"Board's letter," Dell said, and gave it to her and not to me.

She held it at arm's length and then close and then at arm's length again. The wind took the corner of it. She folded it in four and put it in her apron.

"You will want to read that before the water comes," Dell said.

"I will read it when I read it."

He looked at me then, over the barrels. I looked at the pony. The pony looked at the sea. Nobody said what the letter was, and I filled the cans, and the oil ran cold and slow, the color of weak tea, and I got it on my thumb and could not get it off for two days.

When the cart had gone back over the stones she gave me the letter and said her hands were cold. Her hands were not cold. I had felt them on the funnel. I read it to her in the lamp room, slowly, the way the schoolmaster in Saltby had made us read the Lesson, and when I came to the date she said, "Again," and I read the date again.
