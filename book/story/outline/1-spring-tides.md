# Spring tides (arc outline)

*The whole of the first book: from the last of the winter to the inspection at the spring equinox, about six weeks. Wren narrates, first person, past tense. Marks: (Settled …) is the writer's, (Proposal.) is Claude's, (My reading, to confirm.) is Claude's reading of the writer's shorthand.*

## The shape

*(Settled R0302-01.)* Wren covers for Agnes's eyes, takes Agnes's habit for a failing mind, and learns at the crossing that the habit is the one thing Agnes has right. The inspector trusts the printed table, is caught on the causeway, and is brought back by Agnes's forty minutes. Agnes keeps the light one more year, with Wren's eyes.

## The ending

*(Settled R0302-01.)* Agnes keeps the light one more year. The Board never learns of her eyes. Wren keeps the log, and the forty minutes are written in its margin for the next keeper.

## Rules for this arc

- *(Settled R0302-02.)* Wren is right by attention, not brilliance. She notices; she does not deduce aloud.
- *(Settled R0302-02.)* No ghost, no romance, no villain. The danger is the tide and a printed table.
- *(Proposal.)* Adults read Wren by the obvious explanation: when she is late back, she was dawdling; when she watches Agnes, she is homesick.
