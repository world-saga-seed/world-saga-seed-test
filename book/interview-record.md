# Interview record: The Tide Clock

*The intake and interview for the example novel, 2027-03-02. Each answer is written as the ruling it gave, not in the writer's words, and says where it landed. Questions the writer left open are listed last.*

## Existing work

- **What exists:** about 40 pages of a first draft (three scenes), and a page of notes on the island. The scene of the oil cart is the writer's own voice and is kept unchanged as the style sample. Landed: [story/voice/samples/sample-pages.md](story/voice/samples/sample-pages.md), and the voice sheet's samples line.
- **What is fixed and what is free:** the island, the two women, the failing eyes and the inspector's visit are fixed. Everything between is free. Landed: [the key frames](story/notes/1-spring-tides-key-frames.md).

## Scope

- **Length:** one novel of about 70,000 words; a second book is possible but nothing is planned for it. Landed: [plan/state.md](plan/state.md) (the target), the roadmap.
- **Release:** a finished book, not a serial. So the interest check reads the whole book in order, and there is no release plan. Landed: the roadmap.

## Direction

- **The premise in one breath:** an apprentice covers for a keeper who is going blind, and learns that the keeper's strangest habit is the one thing she has right. Landed: the arc outline's head.
- **The ending:** Agnes keeps the light one more year, with Wren's eyes; the Board never learns of the eyes. Ruling R0302-01. Landed: [the arc outline](story/outline/1-spring-tides.md).
- **What it must never become:** a ghost story, a romance, or a story where an adult is saved by a child's cleverness. Wren is right by attention, not by brilliance. Ruling R0302-02. Landed: the arc outline's rules, and the voice sheet.

## Voice and point of view

- **Narrator:** Wren, first person, past tense, told as it happens (no "later I learned"). Ruling R0302-03. Landed: [the voice sheet](story/voice/voice.md).
- **What she notices:** hands, weather, the state of things that need doing. She does not explain the tides; she watches the weed. Landed: the voice sheet, [Wren's sheet](story/notes/characters/wren.md).

## Pacing

- **Where the quiet stretches are:** the lamp work in the middle of the spring. The writer wants them: the book is about a routine, and the routine is where the clue hides. Landed: the arc outline's density notes.
- **What the lulls set up:** each quiet chapter must plant something the inspector's visit pays off. Ruling R0302-04. Landed: the arc outline, and the interest check's reading rule.

## Audience and dislikes

- **Reader:** twelve and up, and adults who read quiet books. Landed: the roadmap.
- **Dislikes:** an adult who explains the plot at the end; a villain where a mistake would do; a tide that behaves the way the plot needs. Landed: the voice sheet, and the setup check's notes in [the arc outline](story/outline/1-spring-tides.md).

## How the writer reads

- **Reads long text on an ereader,** and loses the thread at an unmarked jump in time. So every chapter opens on a day or a tide. Ruling R0302-05. Landed: the voice sheet.

## Open
